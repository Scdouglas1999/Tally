How it works

Four parts, one picture.

Your live TV sources, a public scoreboard feed, the Tally plugin inside your Jellyfin server, and the apps on your screens. The server does the thinking, so every screen in the house sees the same games, the same scores and the same smooth stream.

The diagram

01 · Your sources

Live TV you already have

A paid IPTV service's M3U playlist and XMLTV guide, an HDHomeRun or antenna tuner's lineup, or your own HLS streams. Tally brings no channels of its own.

02 · The scoreboard feed

Scores, clocks, situations

A public scoreboard feed, read by your server, never by the apps. One cached request per league, and only while someone has Tally open or a recording is due.

03 · The Tally plugin

Inside your Jellyfin

Turns sources into channels with a guide, matches games to channels, ranks streams, builds Tally Pulse, records games, and proxies every stream with signed addresses.

04 · Your screens

Every app, same picture

Tally on Android TV, Fire TV, phones, Samsung and LG. The Tally look in Jellyfin web. Live channels and game cards in every other Jellyfin app.

RundownA game night, step by step

What happens between the first pitch and your screen.

  1. Your sources become channels

    The plugin reads your playlists and guides on a schedule. Entries that carry the same thing, like an HD and an FHD copy of one channel, become one channel with several streams. Each stream is probed once: its renditions, how fresh its playlist is, one timed download, and fifteen seconds of watching how steadily new video arrives.

  2. The scoreboard comes in

    While someone has Tally open, the server reads the scoreboard: one cached request per league, about every 12 seconds while a game is live. Every app in the house shares those same requests.

  3. Games meet channels

    Each game is matched to the channels that carry it: by a channel named after the matchup, by what the channel's guide says is on, or by the network. Your guide gets real entries, "Foxes at Gulls" instead of "Live", and every channel a card with its game.

  4. You press Watch

    The app plays the channel's address on your server. Behind it, the plugin serves one continuous stream of its own through a signed proxy, fetching the next few seconds of video ahead of the player into memory, so the player's next request is already waiting.

  5. A stream stumbles, the ladder steps in

    If the stream in use slows, fails or starts arriving in bursts, the next segments come from the next healthy stream, rewritten to continue the old one's timeline. The player never sees a break. After three stable minutes it steps back up.

  6. Pulse and the DVR ride along

    Tally Pulse cuts between the games' own continuous streams, and recordings save the same stream viewers share, so neither adds traffic to your sources.

Inside the plugin

What the plugin adds to Jellyfin.

Live TV

A tuner and a guide

The plugin registers itself as a Jellyfin Live TV tuner with an XMLTV guide, so Tally's channels appear in Jellyfin's Live TV on every client, with Tally Pulse as channel 1.

Security

A signed proxy

Every stream plays through addresses signed with a secret your server makes on first run, so the proxy can't be used as an open relay. Headers your provider needs are added for you.

Web

The Tally look and Sports page

Added to Jellyfin's web client as the page is served. No Jellyfin files are changed, and each is a switch in the plugin's settings.

TVs

The Samsung and LG app

A small shell installed once on the TV loads Tally from your server, so a plugin update updates every TV. For LG, the server also keeps Developer Mode renewed.

Friends

An install page

A link and QR code that walk a friend through installing Tally. The copy of the app your server hands out carries your server's address inside it, and the address is never published anywhere.

Recordings

A game-aware DVR

Starts when the game starts, stops when it's final, remuxes with Jellyfin's own ffmpeg into MP4 with no transcode, and adds it to a library.

On the wire

What leaves your server, and what doesn't.

Tally has no service of its own. Nothing is sent to the developer, by the apps or by the plugin.

  • Your sourcesThe plugin fetches your playlists, guides and streams, with the headers you set for them.
  • ScoreboardRequests for today's games, only while someone has Tally open or a recording needs them. Switch live scores off and the server makes none.
  • Team artBrowsers showing the Sports page load team logos from the scoreboard provider's image server.
  • GitHubJellyfin checks Tally's plugin repository for updates. The plugin keeps a copy of the newest app for the install page, checked every 10 minutes. Apps installed outside a store check for a newer release.

Running the server and want the details? The plugin's README covers every setting, endpoint and log line.
